# Every Way GTA 6's Cops Are Smarter Than GTA 5's — narration only

Read at a natural, quick pace. Section names are for you, not to be read. At ~180-195 words per minute this runs about 14-15 minutes.


## Cold open

In GTA 5, you can rob a store, turn one corner, and the cops forget you exist.

In GTA 6, they remember your shirt.

In GTA 5, a hat was a disguise. In GTA 6, a hat is a hat.

In GTA 5, you could respray the car and drive past the cop who was chasing it.

In GTA 6, the car might be reporting you itself.

In GTA 5, every cop in the city was a dot on your minimap. In GTA 6, there are no dots.

And in GTA 5, the worst thing that could happen was five stars. In GTA 6, it's six, and a dog.

That last one isn't a joke. It happened in Rockstar's own demo, to Rockstar's own studio head.

One review put it simply: the police are "more attuned to your actions than ever."

So I went through every preview, the Rockstar interviews, the reviews, and the strategy guides people wrote in the first week, and I lined up every police feature against how GTA 5 did it.

Here's the thing. These aren't separate features. Every one of them feeds the next one.

And there's one thing GTA 5 gave you in every single chase that GTA 6 took away completely. We'll get there.


## Chapter 1: The Report

Let's start at the very beginning of a crime: the moment the police find out.

In GTA 5, you punch a stranger in an empty field outside Sandy Shores, and a star appears.

Nobody saw it. Nobody called. The game just knew.

To be fair, GTA 5 had a rough version of witnesses. Steal a car in front of someone and they might pull out a phone.

But once those stars showed up, every cop in Los Santos knew exactly where you were. And nothing about who you were.

There was no such thing as getting away with it quietly. There was only getting away.

GTA 6 flips that. According to Rob Nelson, the co-head of Rockstar North, a crime only gets reported if someone sees it, or an alarm goes off.

Someone sees it. Or something hears it. That's it.

Previews add a third: some stolen cars carry a tracker, and the tracker makes the call for you.

Which means, for the first time, there's such a thing as a clean job.

Think about how many GTA 5 missions started with you walking in the front door of somewhere with a gun out. In GTA 6, the front door is the alarm.

No witness, no alarm, no tracker: no cops. Rockstar's word for a criminal like that, as we'll see later, is "Professional."

Of course, in a city this crowded, "no witnesses" is doing a lot of work.

Because now the question isn't whether the police know. It's who told them.


## Chapter 2: The Witnesses

Part two. The witnesses.

In GTA 5, a witness was a pedestrian who screamed and ran in a straight line until they hit a lamppost.

In GTA 6, witnesses are the police's main source of information. Them, and cameras.

If this sounds familiar, Rockstar has built it once before. In Red Dead Redemption 2, a witness would run off to report you, and you could chase them down before they got there.

Wear a bandana, and they'd have a harder time recognising you. Pre-release reporting said GTA 6's police would be much closer to Red Dead's than to GTA 5's.

If you watched the first trailer back in December 2023, you already knew this. Half of it was people filming each other. Phones up, at every fight, every crash, every man in a convenience store doing something he shouldn't.

That wasn't just a joke about Florida. That's the police's evidence.

Previews and guides describe CCTV as one of the things police use to identify you, and it shows up right next to your wanted stars as its own icon.

Here's where it gets clever. Strategy guides written after launch say on-site cameras can be destroyed. Shoot the camera, the footage is gone.

But some cameras upload to the cloud. And you can't shoot the cloud.

The guides' advice for cloud footage is the least GTA sentence ever written: leave the area.

This isn't completely new information, by the way. A leaked development build back in 2022 already showed civilians relaying details to the police: what the suspects looked like, the colour of their car.

Rockstar never talks about the leak, but four years later, that's roughly the system they shipped.

So a witness sees you. A camera sees you. And then they tell the police something very specific.

What you look like.


## Chapter 3: The Description

Part three. The description. And this is the one everybody noticed.

When the police in GTA 6 know what you're wearing, a little coat hanger appears next to your stars.

It's the most threatening coat hanger in video game history.

It's a smart choice, by the way. A face is hard to read from across a street. A bright shirt you can spot from a helicopter.

In GTA 5, clothes did nothing for the police. You could rob the Vangelico jewelry store in a tuxedo, and no one would ever mention the tuxedo.

Masks were the closest thing GTA 5 had to a disguise, and the cops ignored those too. A mask was a fashion choice.

In GTA 6, during IGN's demo, Rob Nelson tried the classic move. Mid-search, he put on a baseball cap and sunglasses.

And his verdict was: "they still know what I'm wearing."

He was asked about haircuts too. A new haircut changes your appearance, he said, but the police still know your clothes.

So: the hat doesn't work. The glasses don't work. The haircut doesn't work.

What works is a whole new outfit. From a clothing store, or, and this is the clever bit, from the trunk of your own car.

Rockstar has basically invented the getaway wardrobe. Remember that trunk. It comes back.

Picture the routine. Rob the place, drive two blocks, pop the trunk, and come out as a completely different tourist.

One catch, according to guides: changing clothes removes that one identifier. It doesn't automatically clear the stars.

Because the coat hanger isn't the only icon. Edge's preview describes others: whether you're carrying a visible weapon, and whether you were seen alone or with your partner.

That last one is new for the series. Jason and Lucia are a couple, so the description is a couple.

Split up, and you no longer match.

In GTA 5, Michael, Franklin and Trevor could commit crimes side by side all day, and the cops never once noticed there were three of them.

Drop the gun, change the shirt, split up. It's a checklist.

But there's one thing on that list you can't fit in a trunk.

The trunk itself.


## Chapter 4: The Car

Part four. The car.

In GTA 5, your car was disposable. Stolen, wrecked, or lost a chase? Take another one.

And if you liked it, Los Santos Customs would respray it, and the heat was gone.

In GTA 6, the car is part of your description. Coverage of the wanted system lists your vehicle right alongside your clothes and your face.

And remember those trackers. Previews say some stolen cars are fitted with one.

Which turns car theft into a puzzle. Steal the wrong car, and you've just stolen a witness.

And Edge reports that cars in GTA 6 run on fuel. So the car you stole for the getaway might not have the range for the getaway. A long chase in a thirsty car is its own kind of wanted level.

It also means the trunk wardrobe from a minute ago comes with a cost. Your spare outfit is in the one object the police might already be looking for.

There's more here that isn't confirmed. Leaked footage showed a traffic camera flashing a car as it sped past, and people read it as a plate scan.

Rockstar has never said that. So for now: maybe.

Commentators have compared the idea to real plate-reading cameras, the kind American cities have been installing for years. Rockstar has always liked its satire close to home.

But here's what is confirmed. The car is also how you run.

And the police in GTA 6 have new ways to make you stop.


## Chapter 5: The Pursuit

Part five. The pursuit.

First, the number. GTA 5 stopped at five stars. GTA 6 goes to six.

That's not a new idea, it's an old one coming back. GTA 3, Vice City, San Andreas and GTA 4 all had six. GTA 5 is the one that cut it.

What actually shows up at six stars, Rockstar hasn't spelled out. The guides disagree, so I'm not going to pretend.

What we do know about is the spike strip. And the dog.

Here's the story, from Edge magazine's preview.

Rob Nelson is playing. Jason and Lucia are on a motorcycle at night, running from the police.

The police lay a stinger strip across the road. At night, it's almost invisible.

The bike goes down. Jason and Lucia are on foot.

And then a police dog takes Jason to the ground.

Edge reports that the developers watching urged Nelson, quite firmly, not to shoot the dog.

In GTA 5, the police tactics were: drive at you. Then, if that didn't work, drive at you harder.

Sometimes a helicopter. Sometimes a roadblock that you could go around on the pavement.

And the classic GTA 5 complaint: police cars that seemed to appear out of nowhere, directly in front of you, as if the city was spawning them just to ruin your day. Because it kind of was.

Reviewers say roadblocks and air support are back in GTA 6, but now they're part of a system that knows what you look like and what you're driving.

And the dog is the perfect example of why the description matters. A car you can swap. A dog follows you.

Which brings us to the best part of any GTA chase. The part where you break line of sight.


## Chapter 6: The Search

Part six. The search.

In GTA 5, losing the cops was a simple deal. Get out of their sight, stay out of their sight, and the stars start flashing. Wait, and they disappear.

It didn't matter what you did. It didn't matter where you went. Just that they didn't see you go.

Before that, GTA 4 did it with a circle. Your wanted level drew a big ring on the map, and you just had to drive out of it.

GTA 5 swapped the circle for those cones of vision. Same idea, prettier geometry.

And the tricks were legendary. Quick round.

Park under a bridge.

Drive into a train tunnel.

Dive into the ocean and swim down.

Sit in a car with the lights off in someone's driveway.

Or just type in a cheat code on your phone.

Every one of those works by beating the cops' eyes. None of them changes what the cops know.

GTA 6 has a search too, but post-launch guides describe more than one kind of star.

Hollow stars: units are responding, but they don't have eyes on you yet. Solid: they're chasing you. Flashing: they've lost you and they're searching.

And then a fourth state the guides describe in red: the chase is over, but the heat isn't.

That heat stays tied to the area. Remember the empty field from the start of this video? In GTA 6, that's exactly where you want to be.

According to the guides, that's where the descriptions come in. The stars measure how hard they're looking. The icons measure what they're looking for.

Lose the stars, and they've stopped chasing. Lose the icons, and they've stopped knowing.

It's the crowded block you robbed that stays hot. The street you hit this afternoon is not the street you want to go shopping on tonight.

So the old tricks might still help with the first part. Hide under a bridge, sure. But you come out from under that bridge in the same shirt.

And some of it, you can't lose.


## Chapter 7: The Profile

Part seven. The profile.

Remember "Professional"?

GTA 6 has something Rockstar calls a Criminal Profile, and it doesn't measure how much crime you commit. It measures how you commit it.

Rob a store without being seen, leave no witnesses, ditch the car properly: that's a Professional.

Shoot your way through the same store, and the needle moves the other way.

People have compared it to Red Dead Redemption 2's honour system. Rockstar's version has one big difference.

Push it far enough, and the profile shatters, and according to reports, it can stay that way for the rest of your playthrough.

Previews also say Jason and Lucia are tracked separately. So one of them can be the professional, and the other one can be the problem.

Rockstar has also said the profile shapes how the story unfolds, without saying exactly how. So this isn't just a number on a menu.

How exactly the profile changes the police is still being worked out. Some write-ups say a cleaner record makes escaping easier; others say there's no direct link to the stars at all.

But think about what that means. GTA 5 had no memory. Every chase started from zero.

In Red Dead 2, at least, you could pay off your bounty at a post office and start clean.

And the very first shot Rockstar ever showed of GTA 6, back in that first trailer, was Lucia in a prison uniform. They told us what this game was about before we saw a single car.

GTA 6 has a record.


## Rumour pile

Now, the stuff that isn't confirmed. Here's every police rumour, and exactly how solid it is.

The traffic cameras reading plates. Seen in leaked footage, never confirmed by Rockstar.

Stolen cars having VIN numbers you'd need to remove. Based on a message that showed up in GTA Online. GTA Online isn't GTA 6, so treat it as a theory.

Six stars covering the whole map with heat that never fades. Some guides say it; nobody official has.

And a shattered profile that won't let you surrender. One guide claims it, others disagree.


## Flip side

So what did GTA 6 take away?

The magic hat.

The respray that fixes everything.

Waiting in an alley with the engine running until the stars blink out.

And the big one.

The police aren't on your minimap anymore.

For thirteen years, every GTA 5 chase was a little game of watching red and blue dots and their little vision cones, and steering around them.

Rob Nelson said it plainly: the cops are off the map. You have to watch the street.


## Ending

"More attuned to your actions than ever."

That's the whole change in one sentence. In GTA 5, the cops chased where you were. In GTA 6, they chase who you are.

And every one of these feeds the next.

Someone sees you, so there's a report. The report has a description. The description has your shirt, your partner, and your car. The car gets the chase. The chase gets the dog. And when it's over, the search, and the record.

The shirt a witness remembers. The partner you had to leave on the other side of the block. The car with a tracker you didn't check for. The dog. The red stars over a neighbourhood you can't go back to. And a profile that remembers all of it.

Is that a worse game? It could be. It's great until you spend ten minutes changing outfits instead of causing chaos. It's great until a police dog ends the best chase you've ever had.

But the best GTA 5 moments were never on a feature list. They were the chase that got out of hand.

And GTA 6 just gave every chase more ways to get out of hand.

So, one question. You've got six stars and one move left: change the shirt, split up, or ditch the car. Which one are you doing?

GTA 6 is out now. Go change your shirt.
